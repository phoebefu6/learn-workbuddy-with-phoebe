Tasks that run because the clock says so 7 min live

A scheduled task is an ordinary WorkBuddy task with two extra decisions: when it fires - hourly, daily, weekly, or one-time - and where the result goes - pushed to Slack, Telegram, Discord, WeChat Work, Feishu, DingTalk or QQ, so the deliverable arrives where your team already looks instead of waiting on your desktop. The official Daily Briefing practice case is the canonical shape: a recurring instruction gathers the same sources on the same cadence and pushes one digest to one channel. Everything you schedule tonight and forever is that shape wearing different clothes.

LiveMatching cadence to task shape4 min▶

The cadence is a design decision, not a preference - each one suits a different task shape:

  • Hourly - monitors and watchers: "flag any new file landing in the shared folder." High frequency means the output must be near-zero-noise or it becomes channel spam by lunchtime.
  • Daily - digests: the official Daily Briefing case. Same sources, one page, every morning.
  • Weekly - briefings and reports with enough new data to be worth reading. Meridian's Monday briefing lives here.
  • One-time - a deadline you refuse to hold in your head: "Friday 4pm, check the pack folder and report status." Fire once, done.

The push target matters as much as the cadence: a briefing pushed to the team's Slack channel is a shared artifact; the same briefing on your desktop is a private chore you still have to forward. In b9, these same chat platforms run the other direction - triggering tasks from your phone.

Self-studyWhat a schedule does NOT change2 min read▶

A scheduled task still runs in the sandbox, still touches only authorized folders and wired connectors (b7), and still writes an audit trail on every run. The boundary from b1 holds. The one thing missing is you: nobody reads the plan at 7am. Every safeguard that remains is either structural (sandbox, folder scope) or baked into the instruction itself (the verify ask). That is why Part 2 exists.

Part 2 · the promotion rule

Only proven instructions get a schedule 6 min live

The rule is one sentence: automate only tasks whose instruction you have already run manually three or more times with clean results. Three clean manual runs prove the instruction survives contact with real data variation - a week with a missing file, a sheet with an extra column, a source that moved. And the stakes are higher than they look, because the verify problem compounds on a schedule: a wrong number you ship once is an incident; a wrong weekly number ships weekly, builds a track record, and gets trusted more each Monday it goes unchallenged. b2's currency trap as a scheduled task would have delivered S$102,947.00 to leadership every week until someone audited.

⚠ Permission modes matter most right here Every task since b1 had you at the plan gate - reading the steps, catching the wrong source, refusing the odd grant. A scheduled task runs without you there. That is not a reason to avoid scheduling; it is the reason for the two compensating controls: only instructions that have earned trust through three clean manual runs get promoted, and the verify clause stays inside the instruction so the checking happens even when you are asleep. Check your Permission Modes before tonight's build-along: scheduled runs should operate under the conservative settings you set in b1, not under something loosened "to make automation smoother". Smoothness is exactly the wrong goal for an unattended write to your files and your team's Slack.
The 5-minute Friday habit The moment anything is scheduled, audit trail review stops being optional and becomes a small weekly ritual: open the scheduled task's history, skim what ran, what it read, what it wrote, and spot-check one figure in the pushed briefing against its source file. Five minutes, once a week, standing between you and the compounding-error problem. b10 turns this into the full shipping discipline; tonight it just goes on your calendar.

Meridian's Monday briefing, scheduled ★ 14 min · everyone does it

You have run b2's five-lever briefing instruction by hand since the simulator session - the one-pager that reports Q2 at S$46,332.00, +23.1% over Q1, top SKU Pandan Green. It has its three clean runs. Tonight it gets promoted.

Retrieve the exact instruction from b2's homework - goal + audience, files by name from ~/Documents/Meridian, one-page format, and the verify ask: "verify every figure against the source files and flag any currency mixing before delivering." Do not rewrite it tonight; the words that earned trust are the words that get scheduled.

Open Automation and create a scheduled task from that instruction. Cadence: weekly, Monday, 7:00am. The saved instruction is now a template - frozen, not improvised per run.

Set the push target: Slack. Pick the channel where Meridian's stakeholders actually look. The briefing becomes a shared Monday artifact, not a file waiting on your desktop.

Confirm the verify ask is IN the scheduled text. Read the saved template one more time and find the clause. That clause is the whole point of this session: on Monday at 7am, it is the only checker in the room.

Test-fire once now. Run the scheduled task manually tonight and check the push lands in Slack formatted and complete. Never let a schedule's first-ever run be the unattended one.

A one-time scheduled run ★ 6 min · everyone does it

The other end of the cadence menu: a deadline task that fires exactly once, so your head does not have to hold it.

Create a one-time task for Friday 4:00pm: "Check ~/Documents/Meridian for the pack artifacts produced so far (b3 sales analysis, b4 report + competitor matrix, b5 launch deck), and push a one-line status per artifact - present, missing, or stale - to Slack."

Note the shape: read-only, low stakes, output is a status line - the perfect one-time task. Deadline checks, pre-meeting pulls, day-before reminders all fit this slot.

Friday, close the loop: when the status lands, do your first 5-minute audit trail review on the Monday briefing task too. Two schedules now exist; the ritual starts this week.

Real world

Why the pack needed this artifact. Meridian's market entry is not a one-shot report; it is a decision that stays live for months. A briefing that builds itself every Monday means leadership tracks Malaysia on fresh numbers without anyone burning Sunday night on it - and the b10 capstone will audit this very task's trail to prove the automation deserved its schedule.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 25-35 min total

  • Schedule ONE task from your own work - and only one that already has three clean manual runs behind it. If nothing qualifies yet, your homework is the three manual runs, not the schedule. That is the ladder working, not you falling behind.
  • Before saving it, read the instruction once more and confirm the verify clause is inside the scheduled text - not in your head, not in a note, inside the words that run at 7am.
  • Set a calendar reminder for Friday: 5-minute audit trail review - skim what your scheduled tasks ran, read, and wrote, and spot-check one pushed figure against its source. Recurring, weekly, permanent.
  • Watch Monday's briefing land in Slack, then verify one figure against the source file yourself. Trust the schedule the way you trust a colleague: warmly, and with spot checks.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What qualifies an instruction for a schedule?

Five levers make an instruction good; three clean manual runs make it proven against real data variation. And "low-stakes so errors are fine" is how compounding errors get a calendar slot.

2 · Why must the verify ask live inside the scheduled instruction itself?

At Monday 7am you are asleep. The verify problem also compounds on a schedule - a wrong weekly number ships weekly and builds false trust each time. The clause inside the template checks every run, including the ones you never watch.

3 · Anything is now scheduled on your machine. What new weekly habit exists?

Set-and-forget is how a wrong number builds a track record. And rewriting weekly destroys the very thing that earned the schedule - the proven template. Five minutes on Friday keeps the trust honest.
