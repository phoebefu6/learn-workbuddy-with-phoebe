From chatbots to agents

Your team already uses AI that answers. A new class of AI executes: it takes one plain-language instruction, plans the steps, works the files on a computer, and hands back a finished deliverable. Tencent's WorkBuddy - launched in China in March 2026, global from May 2026 - is one of the clearest examples of this class. This session strips the hype and keeps the stakes: what execution AI actually is, where it sits in the 2026 landscape, and why it changes the question you manage - from "is my team using AI?" to "are my team's instructions and verification any good?". You never open the tool in this track. You decide about it.

How the leader track works

Six sessions, all in deciding mode: you never install anything, never type an instruction, never touch a setting. Instead you build the judgment that surrounds the tool - what it is (a1-a2), where it fits your team (a3), what the risks are and how they are contained (a4), how to roll it out (a5), and how to know whether it paid off (a6). Part 1 · covers Tencent's "you say, it does" positioning, capability guide framing

Assistive vs execution AI 10 min live

Tencent's four-word framing - "you say, it does" - is the whole shift. Assistive AI (a chatbot) gives your team advice about the work: how to structure the report, what formula to use, five steps they could take. Execution AI gives your team the work: the report itself, the merged spreadsheet, the renamed folder - as a file, with a record of what changed. The official line captures it: "Not just answers. Finished work." The difference sounds small and changes everything downstream: cost per deliverable, where errors hide, and what you as a leader have to govern.

LiveWhy "execution-driven" changes the management question5 min▶

With chatbots, the management question was adoption: are people using it, is anyone pasting confidential data where they should not. With execution agents the question moves, because the agent now does work that ships:

  • You now manage instructions. The quality of what the agent produces tracks the quality of what your people ask for. A vague instruction produces a polished deliverable about the wrong thing. Instruction craft is a trainable team skill - the operator track's b2 teaches it with a live simulator.
  • You now manage verification. A finished-looking file invites trust it has not earned yet. The team norm you need is simple and non-negotiable: nothing an agent produced leaves the building unverified. a4 turns this into a governance checklist.
  • Adoption still matters - but differently. The risk is no longer "nobody uses it"; it is "people use it on the wrong tasks, or trust it too fast." a3 gives you the triage; a6 gives you the metrics.

One sentence to keep: you used to manage people who did the work; now you also manage the instructions and checks around an agent that does some of it.

Part 2 · covers TechNode launch coverage, PR Newswire (SuperAI 2026)

The 2026 agent landscape 8 min live

Three product classes now share the "AI at work" label, and conflating them is how rollouts go wrong. Chatbots answer. Copilots assist inside one app. Desktop execution agents - the OpenClaw-like class WorkBuddy belongs to - plan and act across your computer: files, documents, spreadsheets, schedules. WorkBuddy launched in China in March 2026, went global in May 2026 at SuperAI Singapore, and runs multiple models under the hood - Hunyuan, DeepSeek, GLM, Kimi, MiniMax - so the model is not the thing you manage. The instruction is.

Self-studyHonest limits - what independent reviews agree on4 min read▶

Before anyone on your team over-promises, read the edges. Independent 2026 reviews and Tencent's own materials converge on four:

  • Outputs need verification before external use. The agent delivers finished-looking work; finished-looking is not the same as correct. This is limit number one in every serious review, and the founding reason for session a4.
  • The global edition is young. Worldwide launch was May 2026; international support and some features still lag the China edition, and UI names drift between updates. Where this course could drift with them, we say "re-verify at workbuddy.ai" - do the same in anything you present.
  • Advanced setups have real complexity. Connectors and custom skills take patience the first time; budget pilot time for it rather than assuming day-one magic.
  • It executes; it does not decide. What is worth doing, for whom, to what standard - that judgment stays with your team. An agent amplifies a well-run team and amplifies a confused one too.
Exercise · pen and paper

The agent-shaped audit ◐ 15 min · no tool

Deciding mode means deciding about your team's actual work, not about demos. This exercise takes fifteen minutes and produces the raw material for sessions a3 and a5.

List five tasks your team actually did last week. Real ones, with names: "Mei consolidated the three regional sales files", not "reporting". Small is fine; weekly chores are gold.

Mark each task against three tests. Agent-shaped tasks pass all three: describable (you could write the ask in two sentences), file-bound (inputs and output are documents, spreadsheets, folders), checkable (someone could verify the result in minutes, not hours).

Count the marks. Most teams find two or three of five are agent-shaped. If you found zero, your list is probably too abstract - go one level more concrete and try again.

Keep the sheet. a3 turns it into a delegate-vs-DIY triage; a5 picks the pilot from it. This one page of paper outperforms any vendor deck you will be shown this year.

Real world

Why start with tasks, not tools. Teams that start from "we bought an agent, find uses for it" stall; teams that start from "here are our five most repetitive file-bound chores" ship value in week one. The audit is the difference - and it costs nothing.

Homework

Before session a2 - and questions to ask your team ◐ 20 min total

  • Finish the agent-shaped audit if you did not in session - five real tasks, three tests each. Bring the sheet to a2.
  • Write the one-sentence definition of execution AI in your own words, out loud, until it survives without notes. You will use it with your board sooner than you think.
  • Ask your team this week - and listen more than you explain:
    • "Which task last week felt like following a recipe - the steps were obvious, only the doing took time?"
    • "Where do you already ask a chatbot for advice, and then do all the manual work anyway?"
    • "What deliverable do we produce every single week that nobody enjoys making?"
  • Optional: skim one piece of the launch coverage (TechNode's March and May 2026 pieces are the crispest) so the timeline in Part 2 is yours, not mine.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Your team already uses a chatbot daily. What NEW capability does an execution agent add?

"You say, it does." The chatbot compresses thinking time; the agent compresses doing time. And C is exactly backwards - a finished-looking file makes verification MORE important, not less.

2 · WorkBuddy's launch timeline, as you would state it to your board:

China-first in March 2026, global from May 2026 (SuperAI Singapore). The leader-relevant consequence: international support and features still lag the domestic edition, and details drift - re-verify at workbuddy.ai before you present.

3 · What is the new management question that execution agents create?

Multi-model switching (Hunyuan, DeepSeek, GLM, Kimi, MiniMax) makes the model a detail, and install counts were the chatbot-era metric. What you now manage is the quality of what goes in and the checking of what comes out.
