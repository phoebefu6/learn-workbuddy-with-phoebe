What the data-analysis family really does 7 min live

The official capability guide is specific: WorkBuddy reads your CSV and Excel files, finds trends, and builds charts and dashboards. Inside the four-stage loop that means Understand parses your question, Plan lists which files and sheets it will read and what it will compute, Act does the merge and the math in the sandbox, and Deliver hands you chart files plus a written analysis - with the audit trail of everything it touched. Your job sits at the same two checkpoints as always: read the plan going in, verify the figures coming out.

LiveWhere data tasks sit in the four-stage loop4 min▶

Data tasks stress the loop in one specific place: Act produces numbers, and numbers fail silently. A wrong topic announces itself; a wrong figure sits there looking confident. So the data family shifts your attention to the two edges:

  • At Plan - check the file list and sheet names in the proposed steps. The most common data failure is the right analysis on the wrong sheet.
  • At Deliver - open the source sheet next to the report and spot-check the headline figures. b2's simulator showed you why; tonight you do it on a real deliverable.
  • The audit trail is your friend - it lists what was read and what was computed. Thirty seconds of skimming it is cheaper than one wrong number in a leadership deck.
Self-studyWhat counts as a data task (and what does not)3 min read▶

The official Data Analysis practice case covers spreadsheets in, insight out. Map your own week onto it:

  • Good fits - merging monthly exports, quarter-vs-quarter comparisons, top-N rankings, trend charts, "what stands out in this file" summaries, recurring dashboards.
  • Needs more than tonight - data living in Google Drive or Notion (that is reachable context after b7's connectors), analyses that should re-run every Monday (b8's automation).
  • Not this family - judgment calls the data cannot settle ("should we raise prices?"). WorkBuddy computes the trend; deciding what it means for Meridian stays with you.

The instruction pattern for data tasks 6 min live

Data tasks reward the levers unevenly. Name every file and sheet - "the sales data" invites the agent to guess among files, and a guess over data is a silent wrong number waiting to ship. State the question, not the chart type - "how did Q2 compare to Q1 and what drove it" gets you analysis; "make a bar chart" gets you a bar chart of whatever it guessed mattered. Pin the one-pager format, and put a verify ask on every figure. Here is tonight's actual instruction, clause by clause:

LiveQuestion first, chart second3 min▶

The most common data-instruction mistake is specifying the visualization instead of the question:

  • Chart-first: "Make a bar chart of the sales data." The agent must guess what comparison matters - by month? by SKU? by quarter? You get a correct chart of an arbitrary slice.
  • Question-first: "How did Q2 compare to Q1, and which products drove the change?" Now the trend chart, the top-SKU table, and the headline number all follow from the question - the agent picks the chart that answers it.

Keep the chart-type words for the format clause, where they belong ("one trend chart" as part of the one-pager). The goal clause carries the question.

Self-studyThe pattern as a fill-in template2 min read▶

Save this - it is b2's template specialized for spreadsheets, and it carries every data task in the rest of the course:

"Analyze [question, audience] using only [file path, sheet names] as [format: length, chart, table, risk], routed to the data-analysis Expert, cross-checking every figure against the source and attaching the audit trail."

  • Ambiguous file names in your folder? Rename them first - one minute of file hygiene beats three rounds of plan corrections.
  • Recurring analysis? Freeze the goal and format clauses, swap only the file path week to week. b6 turns exactly that frozen pattern into a custom skill.

Merge Meridian's sales files ★ 12 min · everyone does it

The course pack you downloaded in b1 put sg-sales-2026.xlsx in your Meridian folder - two sheets, Q1 and Q2, six SKUs from Jade Oolong (S$24) to Golden Chrysanthemum (S$16). Run the merge-and-compute half of tonight's instruction and check your run against the canon numbers below.

Instruct. "Merge sheets Q1 and Q2 of ~/Documents/Meridian/sg-sales-2026.xlsx into one table, compute total revenue per quarter and the Q2 vs Q1 change, chart the monthly revenue trend, and list units sold per SKU for the half."

Read the plan. It should name exactly one file and two sheets. If it proposes reading anything else in the folder, stop and tighten the context clause - do not confirm a plan you would not have written.

Confirm and watch it act. Merge, totals, trend chart - all in the sandbox. Keep your hands off; this is delegation.

Check the canon numbers. Q1 total S$37,630.00 · Q2 total S$46,332.00 · change +23.1% · top SKU Pandan Green, 1,060 units in H1. Your figures should match to the cent.

If they do not match - open the audit trail and find what was read. Nine times out of ten a filter or an extra sheet crept in. This debugging move is the whole reason the trail exists.

⚠ The file you must NOT merge tonight Your Meridian folder also contains the Malaysia pilot file - and it is priced in MYR, not SGD. Merging it into tonight's Singapore analysis would sum RM straight into S$ and inflate the total by 3.3x on the MY line, exactly the silent wrong number b2's third trace showed you (S$102,947.00 vs the correct S$89,715.03). Leave it out of every instruction tonight. That trap is b10's lesson, and you will disarm it there properly - with a verify ask.

The one-pager discipline Leadership reads one page; nobody reads six. If your report came back longer, the format clause was loose - "one-pager" plus a named list of parts (headline, chart, table, risk) is what pins it. Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · The best goal clause for a data task states:

Chart-first instructions get you a correct chart of an arbitrary slice. State the question; the right chart follows from it. Chart-type words belong in the format clause, as part of the one-pager spec.

2 · Your run's Q2 total does not match the canon S$46,332.00. First move?

A mismatched figure almost always means different inputs - an extra sheet, a stray file, a filter. The audit trail lists exactly what was read; that is what it is for. Re-running the same instruction re-runs the same mistake.

3 · Why does the Malaysia pilot file stay out of tonight's merge?

The trap is in the data, not the tool: RM 18,985.00 summed as S$ inflates the MY line 3.3x and nothing fails loudly. b10 disarms it properly with a verify ask - tonight you just refuse to step on it.
