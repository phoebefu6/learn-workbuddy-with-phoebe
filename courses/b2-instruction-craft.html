The five levers of a working instruction 8 min live

Every usable WorkBuddy deliverable we could trace back had five properties in the instruction. Every dud was missing at least one. Learn them as a pre-flight checklist - goal, context, format, expert, verify - and "prompt quality" stops being a vibe and becomes a diagnosis.

LiveOne instruction, rewritten five times4 min▶

Watch one real task acquire the levers one at a time:

  • Bare: "Do something with the sales data."
  • + Goal: "Analyze H1 sales for Monday's leadership sync."
  • + Context: "...from ~/Documents/Meridian/sg-sales-2026.xlsx."
  • + Format: "...as a one-pager: headline number, top product, 3 bullets max 15 words, 1 risk."
  • + Verify: "...and before delivering, cross-check every figure against the source sheet and attach the audit trail."

Each added clause removes one guess the agent would otherwise make on your behalf. The simulator below lets you feel exactly which guess each clause removes.

Real world

The instruction is the spec. Teams that get compounding value from agents keep a shared doc of instruction templates per recurring task - goal and format frozen, only the file paths changing week to week. b6 turns your best instructions into reusable custom skills; tonight produces your first template.

Self-studyWhich lever does WorkBuddy's UI hold?3 min read▶

Map the levers onto product surfaces you met in b1 and will meet soon - the product can hold a lever FOR you, if you use it:

  • Goal - yours alone. No UI can rescue a vague goal; the plan-confirmation screen only reveals the guess it forced.
  • Context - folder authorization (b1) + naming files in the instruction. Connectors (b7) extend reachable context to Drive, Gmail, Notion.
  • Format - the instruction, plus Memory (b9) can hold standing format defaults ("my briefings are always one page").
  • Expert - the Expert Center (b5). 100+ specialists; picking one is a single clause in the instruction.
  • Verify - the audit trail exists on every task (b1); the verify ASK makes the agent use it on itself before delivering. b10 builds the full discipline.

Real world

"It met the spec and still wasn't usable." The most common complaint about agent deliverables is the expert gap - the output satisfies every stated constraint and lacks the craft nobody stated. Naming an Expert is the cheapest clause in the instruction: one phrase buys slide grammar, matrix discipline, or legal-drafting caution. b5 tours the Expert Center properly.

Simulator 3 of 3

The silent wrong number ★ 8 min · everyone runs it

The scariest failure is the one that throws no error. Meridian's Malaysia pilot file is priced in MYR; the Singapore books are in SGD. Without a verify ask, the agent sums RM straight into S$ and ships a pack totalling S$102,947.00 - clean, confident, wrong by 3.3x on the MY line. Turn on Verify ask and watch the cross-foot check catch it: RM 18,985.00 → S$5,753.03, corrected total S$89,715.03, audit trail attached.

The rule this trace burns in Verified work, not just finished work. The trap was in the DATA the whole time - the verify lever did not remove it, it surfaced it before leadership did. Any figure leaving your hands gets a verify ask; b10 makes this the shipping discipline for the whole pack.
The golden set

The 10-task scorecard - your course-long benchmark ★ 8 min · explore

Ten real office tasks, from renaming receipts to assembling the market-entry pack. Start with every lever off and climb the ladder - 2 → 4 → 6 → 8 → 9 → 10 - reading the failure text on every red row. This exact ladder is the operator track's spine: the sessions ahead earn you each lever for real.

All off: 2/10. Only mechanical tasks survive a bare instruction - renames and conversions with no ambiguity to resolve.

+ Clear goal: 4/10. The "do something" tasks land, because there is no longer a guess at the center.

+ Context + files: 6/10 (b1's folder rule, b3-b4's file work). Analyses and comparisons reach real data.

+ Output format: 8/10 (b3-b4). Briefings and matrices arrive in usable shape.

+ Right Expert: 9/10 (b5). The deck acquires craft.

+ Verify ask: 10/10 (b10). The pack ships with the currency trap caught, not shipped.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · The deliverable is beautifully structured but full of [DATA UNAVAILABLE] placeholders. Which lever was off?

Placeholder soup is the context-off signature: the agent knew exactly what to build and could not reach the ingredients. Goal-off looks different - a confident deliverable about the wrong thing.

2 · Why is the missing verify ask the most dangerous lever to skip?

Every other missing lever announces itself - wrong topic, placeholders, wrong shape, no craft. The unverified number fails only when someone downstream checks it. S$102,947 looked exactly as confident as S$89,715.

3 · On the golden set, why do 2 tasks pass with every lever off?

"Convert board-update.docx to PDF" has one possible correct outcome. The levers exist to remove guesses; where there is nothing to guess, a bare instruction is already complete. Match instruction effort to task ambiguity.
