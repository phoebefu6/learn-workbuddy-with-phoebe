Part 0

From shortlist to green light

Most agent rollouts fail the same way: buy fifty seats, announce it in all-hands, measure logins, declare victory or defeat on vibes. The playbook tonight is deliberately small. A pilot exists to answer one question with evidence - do the deliverables this agent produces actually get used by the people downstream? Everything else, including how many people logged in, is noise. Small pilots answer that question faster and cheaper than big ones, and they keep a4's risk boundary easy to hold.

Part 1 · builds on a3's shortlist and a4's risk register

Pilot design: 3 tasks x 3 people x 3 weeks 10 min live

Three recurring tasks from your a3 shortlist, three named people who will run them weekly, three weeks on the calendar. That size is not modesty - it is measurement design. Three tasks give you variety without averaging everything into mush; three people give you signal beyond one enthusiast; three weeks is long enough for the second and third runs, where reuse either happens or does not. The success bar is fixed before day one: each pilot task produces a deliverable that its downstream consumer actually uses, at least twice. Logins, sessions, tasks launched - none of it counts.

LiveAdmin Console governance + the champions rule6 min▶

The Team plan's real product is not more credits - it is the Admin Console: centralized governance over who has a seat, what the shared credit pool is spending, and how usage distributes across the team. For a pilot that means two things:

  • One pane for the burn. The 1,000 pooled Credits are a shared budget, and the console is where you watch it. Check the burn at each weekly checkpoint - a task that quietly eats half the pool in week one is a finding, not a footnote.
  • Seats are governed, not scattered. You add and remove seats centrally, which keeps the pilot's blast radius exactly three people wide.

And who gets those three seats? The champions rule: give seats to the three people who already write good instructions - the ones whose emails need no follow-up questions, whose task briefs a contractor could execute cold. Instruction quality is the single biggest lever on agent output (the operator track spends all of b2 on it, and its scorecard climbs 2 to 10 on levers alone). Champions prove the ceiling; skeptics in the pilot only prove the floor. Convert skeptics later, with the champions' finished deliverables as the argument.

Self-studyMarketplace vetting + when to widen access4 min read▶

Two governance calls arrive the moment the pilot succeeds. Decide them deliberately, not by drift:

  • Skills Marketplace vetting. The marketplace ships 20+ skill packages and runs automatic security scanning on submissions - good, but scanning is a filter, not an approval. Team workflow: (1) a champion nominates a skill with the task it serves; (2) one named reviewer checks publisher, requested permissions, and what data the skill touches; (3) it is trialed inside one scoped pilot folder for a week; (4) only then does it go on the team's approved list. An unvetted skill is a stranger's instructions running with your folder access.
  • Widening folder and connector access. a4's rule was one scoped folder per person. Widen org-wide only when three conditions hold: the pilot's verify discipline is habitual (audit trails actually get read), the Admin Console shows no surprise burn or permission sprawl, and a specific recurring task needs the wider grant - named folder or named connector (Drive, Gmail, Notion, Slack), never "everything, to be safe". Each connector also changes the data picture, so revisit Data Management per connector, exactly as the operator track does in b7.

Plan economics: what a seat actually buys 8 min live

Three tiers, one decision. Free ($0) runs the full core loop and is enough for a champion to prove a task solo - the entire operator course runs on it. Pro (~$9.95/mo) is the individual power tier for heavier personal use. Team (~$40/seat/mo) is the only tier that is an organizational product: 1,000 pooled Credits as a shared budget plus the Admin Console to govern seats and spend. You are not buying a bigger model - you are buying governance and a meterable budget.

Re-verify pricing at workbuddy.ai before you present any of this WorkBuddy's global edition launched May 2026 and plans drift - tier names, prices and credit allowances have already shifted between updates. Treat every number on this page as "as verified 2026-08" and pull the live plans page the week you pitch the budget.
LivePooled Credits: a shared budget, so watch the burn4 min▶

Pooled means exactly what it sounds like: the team's 1,000 Credits are one tank, not per-seat rations. That is a feature - light users subsidize heavy weeks - and a risk, because one credit-hungry task pattern can drain the pool for everyone. Leader habits that keep it a feature:

  • Meter by task, not by person. At each pilot checkpoint, note roughly what each of the three tasks costs in credits. Cost per deliverable is next session's core metric - start collecting it now.
  • Set an alert line. Decide up front what burn rate would worry you (say, half the pool by mid-month) and check the Admin Console against it weekly.
  • Never ration by fear. A pilot that hoards credits produces no evidence. Spend them on the three tasks; the pool exists to be measured.
Exercise · 26-42 in session

Draft your pilot one-pager ◐ 16 min

One page, four blocks, CFO-readable in ninety seconds. Draft it now while a3's shortlist is fresh; polish it this week.

Tasks (3). Name the three recurring tasks from your a3 shortlist. For each: who consumes the output today, and how many hours a week it currently costs.

People (3). Name your three champions - the good-instruction writers - and the one scoped folder each will authorize (a4's one-project rule, unchanged).

Success criteria. Write the week-3 gate verbatim: "each task's deliverable used downstream at least twice, verify discipline held, no boundary incidents." Add your rework tolerance - if outputs need redoing by hand more often than roughly 3 in 10 runs, that is a stop signal (a6 formalizes this).

Budget. 3 Team seats x ~$40 = ~$120/mo plus the 1,000-Credit pool, with your alert line stated - and the footnote "pricing re-verified at workbuddy.ai on [date]". Then send the one-pager to one peer for a cold read before it goes up.

Real world

Why one page. A pilot that needs a deck to explain is already too big. The one-pager format forces the discipline this whole session teaches: few tasks, named people, a pre-committed bar, a small metered budget. If a block will not fit, the pilot is overdesigned - cut, do not shrink the font.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · The pilot's success bar is:

Logins measure attendance and hoarded credits measure fear - neither measures value. The only question a pilot must answer is whether the outputs get used by the people they are for. That bar is written down on day 0 so nobody moves it later.

2 · Who gets the three pilot seats?

The champions rule: instruction quality is the biggest lever on agent output (b2's whole scorecard climbs on it), so champions show you the true ceiling. Skeptics come around later - shown the champions' finished deliverables, not a demo.

3 · What is the Team plan's distinctive product, beyond more credits?

~$40/seat/mo buys governance: one pane for seats, spend and usage across the pooled 1,000 Credits. Nothing at any tier removes the verify discipline - and no tier is sold on model size. (And re-verify the numbers at workbuddy.ai; plans drift.)
