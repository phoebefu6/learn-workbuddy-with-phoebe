Packaged craft, hired by clause 7 min live

LiveWhat "packaged craft" actually buys you4 min▶

Take the four flagship families and name the craft each one carries - this is what arrives with the clause and would otherwise need ten format sentences you would never think to write:

  • Market research - source triangulation, market-sizing discipline, a competitor scan that separates facts from vendor claims.
  • Financial analysis - statement structure, variance framing, the habit of showing the calculation next to the number.
  • Slide design - one idea per slide, headline-as-takeaway, an arc that ends in an ask. Tonight's hire.
  • Legal drafting - defined terms, conservative language, flags instead of confident guesses where the law is jurisdiction-specific.

Notice what these have in common: none of it is content. It is the shape of good work in that trade - exactly the thing b2 showed you cannot fully specify with the format lever alone.

Part 2 · builds on b2's deck trace + independent reviews of agent deliverables

The expert gap: met the spec, died in the room 6 min live

Recall b2's deck trace with the Expert lever off: goal on, context on, format on - and the result was ten slides of 210 words each, report paragraphs pasted into a deck frame. Format met, craft absent. That is the expert gap: the output satisfies every constraint you stated and lacks the craft nobody stated. It is the most expensive failure family because it passes your own checklist review - and fails in front of the audience.

LiveDiagnosing the gap before your audience does3 min▶

The expert gap has a reliable tell: you check the deliverable against your instruction and everything matches - yet showing it to a specialist would embarrass you. Three fast probes for any deliverable in a trade you are not expert in:

  • The squint test - for decks: can each slide's point be read in five seconds? For matrices: is every cell the same kind of thing?
  • The pro-glance test - would someone who does this trade daily see structural habits (takeaway headlines, defined terms, sourced sizing) or just content in the right container?
  • The clause audit - reread your instruction. If it names no Expert and the task belongs to a trade, the gap is on by default.

When any probe fails, the fix is one clause and one rerun - which is why this lever earns its point on the ladder so cheaply. When the task has no trade (renaming files, converting formats), skip the clause: b2's effort-matching rule still applies.

Build-along · pack artifact 4

The Meridian launch deck ★ 22 min · everyone does it

The leadership review is the moment Meridian's Malaysia entry gets funded or shelved, and tonight's deck is the artifact that carries it. You have everything the deck needs on disk already: b3's sales analysis and b4's market report + competitor matrix, all in ~/Documents/Meridian. Four levers on, one Expert hired.

Write the instruction with all four earned levers plus the clause. Pattern: "Build a 10-slide launch deck for the leadership review of Meridian's Malaysia entry, from the b3 sales analysis and b4 market report + competitor matrix in ~/Documents/Meridian, following a situation / opportunity / plan / ask arc, using the slide-design Expert; cross-check every figure against the source files and attach the audit trail."

Read the plan before confirming. Two things to verify at the gate: the plan routes through the slide-design Expert (you will see it named), and it reads the b3/b4 files - not the raw spreadsheets. If either is off, fix the instruction, not the plan.

Let it act, then open the deck and check the canon numbers. Slide 2 (situation) should carry +23.1% - SG Q2 vs Q1 growth. Slide 5 (opportunity) should carry RM 18,985.00 - the MY pilot revenue. If either number differs from your b3 artifact, the verify ask's audit trail tells you where it came from.

Run the squint test on all ten slides. One idea per slide, headline states the takeaway, the ask slide asks for something specific. This is the craft the clause bought - compare it against what the format lever alone produced in b2.

Compare against the b2 simulator's skeleton. Reopen b2's deck trace with all levers on and put its 10-slide skeleton next to your real deck. Same arc, same discipline - the simulator predicted the shape; WorkBuddy filled it with Meridian's real numbers. Pack artifact 4: shipped.

Real world

Why "leadership review" is in the goal clause. The same content for a team standup would be five slides and no ask; for an investor it would open with the market, not the situation. The Expert brings the slide grammar; the goal's audience word decides which arc the grammar serves. Levers compound - none of them substitutes for another.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 25-35 min total

  • The two-Expert diff. Take one real brief from your own work and run it twice - once with the market-research Expert, once with the financial-analysis Expert (or any two plausible picks). Put the outputs side by side and list what differs: structure, cautions, what each one checked without being asked. That difference IS the craft - now you know what the clause buys before you spend it.
  • Run the three self-study Experts from Part 1 - market research on your industry, financial analysis on a budget sheet, meeting-notes on a real transcript.
  • Add the Expert clause to the personal instruction template you saved in b2, and note WHICH Expert next to each recurring task on your list. Tasks with no trade get "none" - that is a valid answer.
  • Browse the Expert Center in-app for ten minutes and write down two Experts you did not expect to exist. The catalog is wider than the four flagships - and it drifts, so what you find is fresher than any course page.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What does naming an Expert add that a longer format clause cannot?

An Expert is packaged craft + defaults, not more compute - and nothing skips the plan gate. You could try to write ten format sentences approximating slide grammar; the clause buys the whole trade's shape in four words.

2 · The deliverable matches every constraint in your instruction, yet a specialist would wince at it. Which quadrant, and which fix?

Spec met + craft absent is bottom-right on the grid: the expert gap. It is dangerous precisely because your own checklist review passes it - b2's 210-words-per-slide deck was its canonical form, and one clause moved it out.

3 · Why does the homework insist on running ONE brief through TWO different Experts?

Craft is invisible until contrasted - same brief, same data, two trades, and the diff shows structure, cautions and self-checks each Expert applied unasked. There is no "best Expert overall", only the right trade for the task.
