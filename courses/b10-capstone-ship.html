Part 0

What shipping means

There is a difference between finished work and verified work, and this course has been building to the moment you feel it in your hands. Finished work is a file that exists. Verified work is a file you would defend in front of your boss's boss - because you know where every number came from, you watched the cross-check run, and the audit trail is stapled to it. Tonight the pack ships. Not because it is done, but because it is checked.

Part 1 · covers the audit-trail behavior from "What WorkBuddy Can Do" + the verify-before-external-use finding

The verify discipline 6 min live

Every WorkBuddy task leaves an audit trail. Since b1 you have skimmed it; tonight it becomes a habit with a shape. For every figure in a deliverable, three questions: which source file did this come from, what formula produced it, and if currencies or units were involved, what rate converted it. And one standing clause in every instruction for anything leaving your hands: "before delivering, cross-check every figure against the source and attach the audit trail." You wrote that clause for the first time in b2. Tonight is why.

LiveWhy the discipline pays exactly when you least feel like it3 min▶

The verify step is most tempting to skip on the deliverable that looks best - clean layout, confident numbers, nothing red. That is precisely the profile of the silent wrong number you met in b2: it fails loudly nowhere and lands on your reputation later. The habit that works is mechanical, not motivational:

  • The clause is in the template, so you never decide whether to verify - it is already asked.
  • The trail gets read, not skimmed, for anything external - two minutes, end to end.
  • One figure gets hand-checked - pick the biggest number and trace it to its source yourself. If the biggest one holds, your confidence in the trail is earned, not assumed.
Part 2 · the running project, converging

Assemble the pack - and meet the trap 7 min live

Five artifacts, five sessions of your life: the sales analysis (b3), the market report and competitor matrix (b4), the launch deck (b5), and the weekly briefing that has been building itself since b8. Tonight one instruction assembles them and adds a combined financial summary. And in that summary sits the trap: my-pilot-mar-apr.xlsx is priced in MYR, not SGD. Sum it naively and the pack totals S$102,947.00 - clean, confident, wrong. The correct total is S$89,715.03, because RM 18,985.00 converts at the 3.30 teaching rate to S$5,753.03. One file. A 3.3x error on the Malaysia line. Invisible in a folder listing.

Self-studyWhy traps like this are normal, not exotic2 min read▶

A currency column with no currency marker is the office-data equivalent of an unlabeled jar. Real folders are full of them: revenue in local currency next to revenue in group currency, units in cartons next to units in pieces, dates in two formats. An agent sums what it is given; so does a tired human. The difference tonight is not that WorkBuddy avoids the trap - it is that a one-clause verify ask makes the cross-check run every single time, which no tired human can promise.

The pack trace: verify OFF, then ON ★ 8 min · everyone runs it

The same trace you first ran in b2 - but tonight you are not learning what the levers are; you are rehearsing the exact run you will do for real in fifteen minutes. Replay it twice: first with Verify ask off, and watch S$102,947.00 ship without a single error message. Then verify ON, and watch the run change shape.

Verify OFF, step through to 📦. Every step looks healthy. The total appears confident and formatted. Nothing anywhere says "wrong". Sit with that for a second - this is what the silent failure looks like from the inside.

Verify ON, replay, and watch for the 🛡 step. The cross-foot check re-adds the pack totals, hits the mismatch, and traces it to the RM column in my-pilot-mar-apr.xlsx. This is the moment the whole course exists for.

Read the corrected 📄 delivery. RM 18,985.00 → S$5,753.03 at 3.30, corrected total S$89,715.03, audit trail attached. The trap was in the data all along; the clause surfaced it before your leadership did.

Part 3 · covers Team plan Credits + the honest limits from independent reviews

Credits sanity, and when NOT to delegate 5 min live

Two last pieces of governance before you ship, and one contract said out loud.

LiveCredits sanity - spend where the ambiguity is2 min▶

Tasks consume Credits (the free tier carried this course; Team plans pool 1,000 Credits across seats at ~$40/seat/month - re-verify at workbuddy.ai, pricing drifts). The operator's rule is b2's effort-matching rule wearing a budget: mechanical tasks are cheap and should stay cheap - one clear sentence, no Expert, no drama. Spend your richer instructions, Experts and verify passes on the ambiguous, high-stakes work where a wrong guess costs real money. If a task's Credit cost annoys you, that is usually a sign it was mechanical enough to script, template, or keep doing yourself.

Self-studyWhen NOT to delegate - the boundary that keeps you valuable3 min read▶

The recap that matters most on your last night:

  • Delegate the mechanical - merging, converting, formatting, compiling, scheduling. No ambiguity, no judgment, perfect agent food.
  • Keep the judgment - what is worth doing, for whom, to what standard, and whether THIS deliverable is good enough to ship. WorkBuddy executes; it does not decide. The whole leader track exists because those decisions are a different job.
  • Never delegate the apology. If a wrong number ships, "the agent did it" convinces no one - the verify gate was yours. Which is exactly why it lives in your template and not in your memory of good intentions.

And the operator's contract, said out loud one last time: one instruction in, one verifiable file out - and nothing leaves your hands unverified. That sentence is the course. Everything else was practice.

The build-along

Ship YOUR pack ★ 15 min · this is the capstone

Not the simulator's pack - yours. The five real artifacts you built across this course, assembled, verified, and sent to an actual human being. This is the fifteen minutes the other nine sessions were for.

Assemble. Create ~/Documents/Meridian/pack/ and gather your five artifacts: the b3 sales analysis, the b4 report and matrix, the b5 deck, and the latest b8 briefing. If one is missing, that session's homework is tonight's homework first.

Run the combined summary - with the clause. "Build a combined financial summary of all files in ~/Documents/Meridian/pack as a one-pager; before delivering, cross-check every figure against its source file, state the formula and any conversion rate used, and attach the audit trail."

Read the plan at the gate - your last plan confirmation of the course. Right files? Right output? You have done this since b1; do it like it is day one.

Read the audit trail end to end. Not a skim - every figure, its source, its formula, its rate. Hand-check the biggest number yourself. If your data hides a trap like the RM column, this is where it surfaces. Find nothing? Then you have earned the confidence, not assumed it.

Then - and only then - send it to someone real. Your manager, a colleague, a friend who will actually open it. Shipping to a real reader is the difference between finishing a course and changing how you work. Press send.

★ The close - what you keep doing weekly The course ends; three small rituals carry it: audit-trail Friday (read one trail end to end, every week), Memory monthly (the b9 audit, already on your calendar), and one new template per recurring chore (every time you type the same instruction twice, save it). You arrived with tasks and a to-do list. You leave with a delegation system, a verification habit, and a scorecard that says 10/10 because you made it say that. Go ship things.

Check yourself

Three last questions 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · The naive pack total was S$102,947.00. What made it wrong?

The trap was in the data, not the arithmetic. Every addition was performed correctly on wrongly-mixed currencies - which is why nothing failed loudly, and why the correct total is S$89,715.03 only after the verify pass converts the RM column.

2 · What are the three audit-trail questions for every figure that ships?

Source, formula, rate - the three answers that make a number defensible in front of anyone. The other lists matter for planning and budgeting, but they cannot tell you whether the figure itself is right.

3 · After this course, which of these should you still NOT delegate to WorkBuddy?

Mechanical work delegates beautifully; judgment does not. What is worth doing, to what standard, and whether THIS file ships - that stayed your job on session one and it stays your job now. It executes; you decide. That is the contract.
